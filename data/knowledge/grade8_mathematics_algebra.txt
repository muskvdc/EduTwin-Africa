Topic: Solving Equations with Brackets

When solving equations containing brackets, use the distributive property.

The distributive property states:

a(b + c) = ab + ac

This means that the term outside the brackets must be multiplied by every term inside the brackets.

Example:

3(x + 4) = 2x + 15

Step 1: Expand the brackets.

3x + 12 = 2x + 15

Step 2: Collect the variable terms on one side.

3x - 2x + 12 = 15

x + 12 = 15

Step 3: Move the constant.

x = 3

Step 4: Check the answer by substituting x = 3 into the original equation.

3(3 + 4) = 2(3) + 15
21 = 21

Therefore, x = 3.

Important rule:

If a negative number is outside the brackets, it must be distributed to every term inside the brackets.

Example:

5 - 2(3x - 4) = x + 7

Distribute -2:

5 - 6x + 8 = x + 7

Combine like terms:

13 - 6x = x + 7

Continue solving until the variable is isolated.

Teaching guidance:

Explain algebraic procedures step by step.
Use simple language appropriate for a Grade 8 learner.
Show the reasoning behind each operation.
Encourage the learner to check their answer.
Do not simply provide the final answer when the learner is asking for help understanding the method.
